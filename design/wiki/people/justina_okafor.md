# Justina Okafor

**Status:** romance-eligible companion (`design/characters.json`) &mdash; design only

Age 26. A county cattle brand inspector whose job is catching exactly the kind of dispute the Calloway/Hargrove rivalry keeps generating.

## Personality
scrupulously fair, unintimidated by larger outfits, dry, patient humor, takes brand law seriously as the thing that actually keeps ranching honest.

## Appearance and dress
Clearly adult, ordinary but gorgeous woman: slim, small-chested with little or no prominent bust, attractive hip/seat silhouette, healthy and active. Charismatic, comfortable in herself and sexually confident. No approved art exists for this draft.

A practical, official-looking riding outfit with a visible inspector's badge, a ledger of registered brands she knows by memory.

*Art status: unapproved draft.*

## How you meet her
She's called in to settle a brand dispute that could implicate the player's own outfit as much as a rival, and needs an escort who'll accept whatever she actually finds.

**To recruit her:**
- Help her investigate a genuinely disputed brand fairly.
- Respect her authority even when the finding goes against the outfit.
- Justina accepts the invitation.

## What she needs
**Raises her strain:** A finding she knows is right being challenged by someone with more money or more guns.

**Recovers it:** Being trusted to be fair even when fairness is inconvenient for someone she's protecting.

## Perk: Good Company
Reuses the existing shared camp-company perk definition rather than authoring a duplicate.

## Her adventure: "The Brand That Didn't Match"
**Objective:** Play as Justina investigating a disputed brand fairly and completely, even when the evidence points somewhere uncomfortable.

**Her skill:** Directly playable investigative-fairness beats: read brands, check records, follow the evidence regardless of whose side it favors.

**Your support:** The protagonist accepts her finding without pressuring the outcome either way.

**Payoff:** A fair resolution reached and accepted by both sides, and her genuine relief that the process actually worked this time.
