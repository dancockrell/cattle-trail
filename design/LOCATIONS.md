# Cattle Trail — gazetteer

Expanded world locations, written 2026-09-10 at Dan's direction to build the world out ahead of rooms existing for all of it. `LORE.md`'s "The country" section stays the short version for the room that exists today; this file is the long version for rooms that don't exist yet. Nothing here is implemented. A location becomes real when a room is built for it, not when it is described here.

Each entry: what it is, why the outfit would go there, and an art-direction comment for whoever paints it later. Follow the palette and staging already proven in the Clear Fork room — warm dusty daylight, fixed high three-quarter view, hard pixel edges — unless the entry says otherwise.

---

## Clear Fork (built)

The starting ground. Wagon camp, east gathering, the dry crossing, the cart stop, the clue stretch. Documented fully in `LORE.md`. Everything below is reached from here, on later trail days.

## Fort Griffin

A hide-and-soldier town on the Clear Fork of the Brazos, real in period fact and real in this world too. Saloons, a trading post, a garrison that mostly minds its own business. The outfit sells here, resupplies here, and hears rumors here. Griffin is loud, crowded, and does not know or care what the outfit has seen out on the grass — that mismatch is the point of any scene set here.

Possible business: selling the herd count, a bounty poster on a face the player recognizes, a trader who wants a "curiosity" the outfit is carrying and should not sell.

## The Narrow Water

A second crossing upriver from Clear Fork, deeper and colder, where the ford is honest but the bank is not. Cottonwood roots and a washed-out cart track. This is a travel encounter location, not a camp: something is usually wrong here before the outfit arrives, and the scene is about reading the signs before committing the herd to the water.

## Bellhollow

A near-abandoned mining camp in scrub hill country, played out a decade ago and mostly returned to the country. A handful of holdouts remain. The name is literal: a bell somewhere in the collapsed workings rings when nobody is there to ring it. This is where a heavier haunting lives — not a single spirit at a crossing, but a place the dead never fully left.

<!-- art prompt: Collapsed mine headframe and a scatter of leaning shacks on a scrub hillside, one window lit at dusk, a bell tower visible but the bell itself obscured. Desaturate slightly relative to Clear Fork to read as a harder, poorer place, without going full horror-palette. -->

## The Calloway Spread

A neighboring cattle operation, larger and better-fenced than the outfit's own, run by a family that has been in this county longer. Not enemies by default — rivals for water, grass, and market timing, which is plenty of friction on its own without inventing villainy. A source of both help and competition depending on how the outfit has treated its neighbors.

<!-- art prompt: A proper ranch house and outbuildings behind good fencing, windmill pumping water into a stock tank, tidier and more established than the outfit's own wagon camp. Same period and palette; this should read as "further along the same road," not a different setting. -->

## Providence

A rail-line town growing fast on the promise of a spur line that may or may not get built. Boomtown energy: fresh lumber, tents next to real buildings, speculators, a church with no steeple yet. A good place for the outfit to feel out of step with where the country is actually heading.

<!-- art prompt: A single dusty main street half-built, raw new lumber next to canvas tents, a locomotive whistle implied rather than shown (no train needs to be on-screen). Bright midday light, busier and more chaotic than Griffin. -->

## The Salt Flats

Dead-white ground south of the usual trail, where nothing grows and sound carries wrong. Cattle refuse to cross it without a reason to trust the drover. One of the places where the country's late signs show clearest — mirages that do not resolve, tracks that end mid-stride. Not a combat arena; a place you get through, not a place you fight in.

<!-- art prompt: A flat white salt pan under a bleached sky, heat-shimmer distortion kept subtle, a single line of wagon tracks vanishing toward the horizon. High-key lighting, almost overexposed, to contrast against Clear Fork's warm palette. -->

## Split Oak Camp

An old line-shack and a single enormous, storm-split live oak that other outfits use as a waypoint and a message board — notices, brands, and warnings nailed to its trunk over the years. Neutral ground by informal custom. A good place for rumor delivery and for meeting travelers going the other direction.

<!-- art prompt: One massive live oak, visibly split by old lightning damage but still alive and full-canopied, a small line-shack in its shade, scraps of paper and carved marks visible on the lower trunk. Warm daylight, generous shade pooling under the canopy. -->

## The Quiet Rows

A small family cemetery from a homestead that failed years back, fenced and still tended by someone nobody in the outfit has met. The graves are ordinary. What is not ordinary is that the count of headstones does not match the count from the last visit. This is a slow-burn location: return visits, not one-time set dressing.

## Using this list

A location earns a room when a companion adventure, a rumor, or a story beat actually needs it — not before. Pick the smallest set of new locations that serves whatever is being built next, the same discipline `GAME-DESIGN.md`'s scope decision already applies to systems. Update this file when a location graduates to a real room, and say so at the top of its entry the way Clear Fork is marked above.
